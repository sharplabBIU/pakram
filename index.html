About the division

Psychology, Cognition, Emotion & Brain

PAKRAM (פק״רם) is the Hebrew acronym for Psychology, Cognition, Emotion and Brain, the research-oriented M.A. track of the Department of Psychology at Bar-Ilan University. Cognitive, emotional and behavioral processes in the brain unfold in continuous interaction, and together they drive human behavior. The division studies those links in depth, from basic theory to applied research.

Students learn to record brain activity in real time, analyze physiological responses and run behavioral experiments with modern sensing technologies, including eye tracking, EEG and MEG, brain stimulation (TMS and tDCS), electrophysiological recording, cutting-edge virtual reality with the Somnium VR1 headset, and computational modelling. Every student completes a thesis in a department lab under the close supervision of a faculty member, and many take part in research projects with partner hospitals and research institutes.

What makes the division distinctive is the pairing of rigorous measurement with formal modeling. Alongside classic cognitive neuroscience, our faculty bring computational psychiatry, social neuroscience, developmental science and the study of emotion into a single program, and ask how advances in artificial intelligence can serve, and be informed by, a science of the human mind.

Our approach

Why multidisciplinary science is the route to mechanism

The division's defining commitment is that rigorous, mechanistic models of how humans think and feel cannot be built from inside a single discipline. A mechanism is a claim about how parts interact to produce a phenomenon, and the parts of human thought and feeling live at different levels: ion channels and brain rhythms, learning rules and value computations, developing concepts, bodies in contact, and the social groups that shape all of them. A model that explains anxiety only at the level of oscillations, or only at the level of beliefs, is not yet a mechanism.

That is why the division deliberately brings computational psychiatry, magnetoencephalography and electrophysiology, developmental science, social neuroscience, neuropsychology and pharmacology into one program. Formal models give neural data a hypothesis to test; neural data constrain models that would otherwise fit behavior equally well; developmental and social perspectives reveal where a mechanism comes from and what it is for. Each discipline supplies a check on the others, and the result is explanation that is both precise and faithful to the complexity of a human life.

For students, this means learning to move between levels: to write a reinforcement-learning model one day, analyze MEG source estimates the next, and ask how both bear on a clinical question or on the design of an artificial agent. This fluency is what separates a scientist who can build a mechanism from one who can only describe a correlation, and it is equally valuable in a research lab, a clinic or an R&D team.

Measure

Record the brain and body directly: MEG and EEG oscillations, brain stimulation, eye tracking, hormones and physiology, and immersive virtual reality that places people in controlled yet lifelike situations.

Model

Turn verbal theories into equations: reinforcement learning, Bayesian and active inference, and machine learning that make quantitative predictions about choices and neural signals.

Situate

Ask where a mechanism comes from and what it does in the world: in childhood, in the dyad and the group, in clinical conditions, and in artificial agents.

Two pathways, one training

Prepared for industry and for academia

The same core skills, careful experimental design, quantitative modeling and the analysis of rich neural and behavioral data, are in demand inside and outside the university. The program is built so that graduates can choose either road, or move between them.

Industry

Into industry and applied R&D

Graduates leave with a portfolio of transferable skills that technology, health and defense employers actively recruit for:

  • Programming and data analysis in Python and R, including machine learning and statistical modeling.
  • Signal processing of EEG, MEG, eye-tracking and physiological data.
  • Computational modeling of decision-making, learning and emotion, the same tools used in AI and behavioral analytics.
  • Experimental design, A/B-testing logic and human-centered evaluation for product, UX and neurotechnology teams.
  • Practica and projects that connect students with hospitals, research institutes and R&D groups.

Department graduates work in medical and clinical research, defense R&D, education and the business sector.

Academia

Into a PhD and a research career

For students who want to stay in science, the division is a direct on-ramp to doctoral research:

  • A full research thesis supervised one-on-one by a faculty member, often leading to a conference presentation or publication.
  • A combined M.A.-to-PhD track for outstanding students who want to move quickly into doctoral work.
  • Access to the Gonda Multidisciplinary Brain Research Center, including its MEG laboratory, and to labs across the department.
  • Advanced seminars on current theory across the division's research areas.
  • Mentoring on grant writing, international collaboration and scientific communication.

In a department survey of track graduates, 85% held research positions.

Research in the division

Current directions

What each of our eight labs is pursuing right now, every one combining measurement, modeling and context in its own way.

Sharp Lab

Computational psychiatry

Mathematical models of learning, planning and decision-making are fitted to people's choices to ask which computations go awry in anxiety and compulsivity, and how they change with treatment. The lab combines these models with behavioral tasks, virtual reality and wearable MEG.

Peled-Avron Lab

The neuroscience of touch

Social touch is one of the earliest and most powerful channels of human connection. The Touch and Neuropsychopharmacology Lab uses EEG, brain stimulation and pharmacology (oxytocin, psychedelics, MDMA) to understand how the brain perceives touch and empathy, and how this changes in autism, depression and PTSD.

Gordon Lab

Synchrony in multimodal measurement

When people interact, their brains, bodies and behavior fall into and out of sync. The Social Neuroscience Lab measures synchrony across physiology, hormones, movement and neural signals in pairs and groups, asking when it helps or hurts cohesion and performance, with the support of an ERC Consolidator Grant.

Diesendruck Lab

The development of beliefs

How do children come to believe that social categories such as ethnicity, religion and gender carry deep “essences”? The Language and Cognitive Development Lab traces how beliefs about the social world emerge in childhood and how they shape intergroup attitudes, moral judgment and cultural learning.

Goldstein Lab

Measuring thoughts and emotions with MEG

Magnetoencephalography captures brain activity millisecond by millisecond. In Israel's only MEG laboratory, the lab tracks the fast oscillations and connectivity that underlie perception, language, emotion and the sense of self, from meditation to the way an audience synchronizes with a speaker.

Anaki Lab

Faces, memory and the aging brain

How does the brain detect and recognize faces, objects and expressions, and what happens to memory in aging, Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease? The lab combines psychophysics, EEG and the neuropsychology of prosopagnosia with studies of semantic networks in creative cognition.

Weller Lab

Early life, stress and feeding

How do early-life experience, maternal care, stress and diet shape emotional and feeding regulation? The Developmental Psychobiology Lab uses rat models of depression, obesity and binge eating, with a focus on gut peptides, oxytocin and cortisol, epigenetics and cannabinoids.

Lavidor Lab

Brain stimulation for language, control and creativity

Non-invasive brain stimulation (TMS, tDCS and tACS) combined with behavioral and EEG methods reveals how the two hemispheres share language, reading, cognitive control and creative thinking, and yields stimulation and training protocols that enhance these abilities.

Human minds and artificial intelligence

Where human cognition meets machine cognition

Artificial intelligence now touches every field represented in the division, from models of learning and decision-making to the analysis of brain, physiological and behavioral data. In our classes we discuss how ongoing developments in AI can help promote our multidisciplinary interests: sharper models of how people think and feel, better tools for rich multimodal data, and new questions about the mind that were out of reach only a few years ago.

The program also prepares students to think carefully about how human cognition and emotion might differ from machine cognition. That question matters to industry, which builds systems that people must work alongside, and to academia, which builds theories of the mind that machine learning both challenges and informs. Graduates leave able to hold both perspectives at once.

A tailor-made path

Your program, built around you

No two students arrive with the same background or leave toward the same destination. From the first semester, each student works with a faculty mentor to shape a plan: a thesis lab, a choice of seminars and electives, and the research experiences that build exactly the skills their path requires.

Choose a lab and a mentor

Applicants rank three potential supervisors from the department list. Once admitted, your supervisor becomes your guide to the program, the thesis and the career decisions beyond it.

Shape your toolkit

With your mentor, choose the seminars, electives and hands-on experiences that fit your direction, whether that pairs computational modeling with MEG analysis or social-neuroscience methods with machine learning.

Produce original research

The thesis is a genuine research project. Many students present at international conferences or publish before they graduate, whether they are heading to a PhD or to industry.

Admissions

Join the division

The track is a two-year M.A. with thesis, taught over two intensive days per week.

Requirements

  • A bachelor's degree in psychology with a grade average of 85 or higher.
  • Two academic or research recommendations.
  • A personal research-admissions interview.
  • Graduates of related fields (neuroscience, cognition, behavior, communication disorders) may apply and complete bridging courses.

Applications for the 2027 academic year are accepted from February 2027 until August 1, 2027. Applicants pay the registration fee on the university site, complete registration in the department system, update recommender details, upload the track questionnaire and rank three potential supervisors from the supervisor list.
